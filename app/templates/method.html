Method / what four samples a second can and can't measure

Measure first, compare later

Before any driver was compared, every step was checked against a known truth: how the public feed samples the car, what filtering does to an event, how accurate each way of finding a braking point is, and how well laps line up. These charts are that evidence (Phases 1–3).

1 the feed

Under four samples a second, on a 40 ms clock

Every interval between two car samples, from the pilot weekend. They come in steps of 40 ms (the bars stand on a comb), mostly 0.16–0.48 s apart, with rare dropouts of up to s. Anything faster than half the sample rate (the Nyquist frequency) can't be seen, and interpolating more points in between adds no information.

3 choosing estimators

The obvious method is the wrong one

Synthetic corners with a known truth were sampled exactly like the feed (random phase, real intervals, whole-unit rounding), times per corner type, and every candidate estimator was scored by its root-mean-square error (RMSE, the typical size of the error, bias included).

5 cleaning

What was kept, and why the rest was not

Rules were fixed on 2023–2024 (exploratory data analysis) and applied unchanged to the held-out 2025 season. A corner pass with a feed dropout inside its window is not used: an event inside a 1-second hole can't be located.
