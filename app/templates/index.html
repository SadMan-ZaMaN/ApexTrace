Can you recognise a driver from public telemetry?

ApexTrace measures where each driver brakes, how much speed they carry to the apex and when they get back on the throttle, corner by corner, in every qualifying session from 2023 to 2025. It uses only the public timing feed, at {{ d.sample_rate_hz }} samples per second. Then it asks how much of that style belongs to the driver, and how much to the car.

ICC (intraclass correlation): the share of the spread that is between drivers rather than lap to lap; 0 = none, 1 = all. p: how often a result at least this strong would appear if there were no real effect. Balanced accuracy: how often the right teammate is named for a lap, 0.5 = guessing. A "not supported" is a result too: it is reported as it came out.

How it works

01

Sample

Speed, throttle, brake and gear arrive {{ d.sample_rate_hz }} times a second on a 40 ms clock, with gaps. Nothing faster than {{ "%.1f"|format(d.sample_rate_hz / 2) }} Hz (half the sample rate) can be seen.

02

Align

Every lap is placed on one track coordinate by fusing car position with integrated speed: {{ d.alignment_error_m }} m along-track error.

03

Find corners

Braking zones are detected automatically on each session's fastest lap and typed slow, medium or fast.

04

Measure

{{ d.features }} features per corner: brake onset, apex, minimum speed, throttle pickup, exit speed and more, each with a known error.

05

Test

Leave-one-weekend-out identification against baselines, and preregistered tests on a season kept locked until the end.
